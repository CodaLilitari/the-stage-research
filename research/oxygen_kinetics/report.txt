MODEL AND UNITS
A carrier alternates between loading and unloading baths held at fixed oxygen pressures. During each phase:
  dS/dt = k_phase * (S_equilibrium - S).
Hill equilibrium saturation is S(P)=P^n/(P^n+P50^n). For eL=exp(-kL*tL) and eU=exp(-kU*tU), the cyclic steady-state saturation change is:
  deltaS = [(1-eL)(1-eU)/(1-eL*eU)] * [S(PL)-S(PU)].
The phase endpoints follow the exact affine cycle-map fixed point when kL*tL+kU*tU>0.

B*deltaS is bound-oxygen amount per carrier volume per cycle (mol/m³/cycle). Dividing by tL+tU yields a volume-normalized exchange rate (mol/m³/s). It is not an area flux, whole-organ oxygen delivery, tissue uptake or a prediction of survival. At cyclic steady state, uptake and release by the carrier agree because its oxygen inventory returns to its starting value. The baths are maintained by external sources/sinks; the combined system is not a closed finite oxygen reservoir. Finite-bath depletion, dissolved oxygen, membranes, flow distributions, tissue consumption, heat, pH, allostery and biological stability are outside this model.

Frozen hypothetical sweep values: PL=100 mmHg, PU=10 mmHg, Hill n=2, B=5 mol/m³, tL=tU=0.8 s, kL=2.5/s. Constant case kU=2.5/s. Dependent case kU=2.5*(P50/sqrt(PL*PU))/s. P50 values 5 through 80 in integer steps. The dependent rate is an assumption about effective unloading relaxation, not a measured molecular off-rate.

AFFINITY DOES NOT IDENTIFY AN OFF-RATE
The returned prose said higher affinity fundamentally comes from slower unbinding. That is false as a general statement. Define a separate, noncooperative one-site toy model:
  dS/dt = kon*P*(1-S) - koff*S.
Direct algebra gives P50=koff/kon and relaxation k(P)=kon*P+koff. Higher affinity can therefore arise through larger kon at unchanged koff. At nonzero unloading pressure, relaxation kU is not just koff.

Executed counterexamples use PL=100, PU=10, n=1, B=5 and tL=tU=0.1 s; these differ deliberately from the n=2 main sweep and are not fitted biological constants:
Case                         kon        koff     P50     kU       q per volume
Reference                    0.1        2         20       3        0.582845
Faster association           0.2        2         10       4        0.647735
Both rates ten times faster  1.0       20         20      30        2.375518
kon units are (mmHg*s)^-1; koff and kU are s^-1; P50 is mmHg; q is mol/m³/cycle. In the second row affinity increases without slowing koff, and effective unloading relaxation is faster. In the third row equilibrium affinity is unchanged while finite-contact exchange increases markedly. Equilibrium affinity alone therefore does not determine dynamic throughput, even in this simple model.

CORRECTIONS AND LIMIT CHECKS
The original denominator threshold and subtraction of near-identical exponentials erase tiny positive contact effects. The corrected formula uses expm1 and computes deltaS directly from the attenuation factor. At equal k=1/s and tL=tU=1e-18 s, per-cycle exchange is positive but tiny; dividing by the tiny cycle period gives the finite fast-cycling limit B*deltaS_equilibrium*k/4. This mathematical limit assumes zero transfer/transit overhead and is not an achievable pump specification. Shrinking just one contact time while keeping the other fixed instead drives exchange and the volume-normalized rate to zero.

If both integrated rates are zero, every initial saturation is a fixed point; the implementation returns no unique endpoint rather than inventing one. For a zero-length cycle, an exchange rate per time is undefined. With equal bath pressures, exchange is zero and normalized efficiency deltaS/deltaS_equilibrium is undefined; the implementation keeps this separate from the algebraic attenuation factor. Zero capacity, zero contact, fast equilibration, the equal-rate tanh identity, pressure-unit invariance, seconds-to-milliseconds conversion, linear capacity scaling and invalid-input rejection were tested.

Independent numerical RK4 integration was performed for four frozen parameter sets from initial saturation 0 and 1. Its integration step was bounded by k*dt<=0.02 with at least 20 steps per phase. The cycle count was chosen from the exact map's contraction bound to reduce initial-state dependence below 1e-13; it was not arbitrarily fixed at 40 cycles for all rates. Across eight comparisons the maximum endpoint error was 2.3271421e-10 and maximum loading-minus-unloading imbalance was 3.2751579e-15. These checks validate the implemented equations within the chosen cases; they do not validate the equations as biology. Within-project independent implementation QA passed 179/179 checks: experiments/independent_qa/ok0_run_1792074813960296800/report.json. A separately written Decimal fixed-point calculation reproduced all 152 sweep rows within 2.22e-15; an analytic derivative with bisection gave the dependent optimum 41.2997705706, matching the bounded search. Eight saved RK4 comparisons were verified. The reviewer also reproduced the original tiny-contact defect and verified the correction through contact times of 1e-20 s.

Numerical scope correction: the frozen source comment describing arbitrarily slow relaxation is too broad. At contact times near 1e-200 s, multiplying two attenuation numerators underflows in double precision and can incorrectly erase the finite fast-cycling rate. No declared sweep or numerical test uses that regime. The source remains preserved; use the demonstrated numerical range rather than interpreting its comment as a universal guarantee. This model represents one carrier parcel alternating between clamped phases, not two finite well-mixed continuous-flow stirred tanks.
