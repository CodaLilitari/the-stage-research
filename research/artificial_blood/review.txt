THE STAGE: ARTIFICIAL BLOOD, PERFUSION AND A FIRST TRANSPORT RESULT
Coda's local assessment for Richard Pemberton | 15 October 2026

Recommendation
--------------
For the stated destination of living tissue over a robotic body, the most direct
near-term research object is a perfused, replaceable living-tissue module with
an external support loop. That is an engineering judgment about this project's
dependencies, not a prediction that building it will be easy. Human transfusion
oxygen therapeutics are a serious established research field, but developing one
would add a separate clinical product problem before solving the tissue module's
transport problem. Neither route starts with an all-purpose replacement for blood.

Three different things currently share the name "artificial blood"
---------------------------------------------------------------
1. HUMAN OXYGEN THERAPEUTIC. A carrier intended to transport oxygen in a human
   circulation must be evaluated in that biological environment. The 2022 HbV
   first-in-human study assessed safety and pharmacokinetics in healthy adults;
   reported infusion reactions and fever limit any claim of simple readiness.
   It was not a demonstration of whole-blood replacement or clinical rescue
   efficacy. The paper is historical evidence, not a current approval inventory.
   Source: Azuma et al., Blood Advances 2022, DOI 10.1182/bloodadvances.2022007977.
   https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9618780/
   Read depth: primary indexed text covering rationale, trial design and adverse
   findings; direct PMC opening was challenged. No dosing or preparation advice
   is inferred here. The parent assessment separately checks current trials.

   FDA's 2018 research account emphasizes that HBOCs differ biochemically and
   that oxidation/reactivity can compromise both oxygen transport and safety.
   Capacity alone is therefore an inadequate product specification. Its dated
   regulatory statement must not be presented as an October 2026 status check.
   https://www.fda.gov/vaccines-blood-biologics/science-research-biologics/fda-comparison-characteristics-hemoglobin-based-oxygen-carriers-could-guide-development-safe-and
   Read depth: FDA research summary of its comparative biochemical study.

2. ENGINEERED-TISSUE PERFUSATE. The target is a complete support system: a fluid,
   delivery network, exchange surfaces and regeneration/removal functions. A
   bench-supported tissue patch need not simultaneously reproduce every human
   blood function. Oxygen delivery still depends on flow, distance, wall transfer
   and consumption; total carrier capacity cannot substitute for those terms.
   Nutrient/waste transport and the fluid's compatibility with the selected tissue
   remain separate required questions. The PF0 run below addresses oxygen only.

   Grebenyuk et al. (2023) demonstrated synthetic microfluidic networks supporting
   neural and liver constructs at multi-mm scale. Their common grid was
   2.6 x 2.6 x 1.5 mm with 250-micrometer inter-vessel spacing. This establishes
   experimental precedent for an engineered delivery architecture, not a living
   skin envelope over moving machinery or a complete artificial circulation.
   The authors explicitly distinguish it from adaptive natural vessels and
   selective blood-brain-barrier interactions.
   https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-022-35619-1
   https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9837048/
   Read depth: primary abstract, geometry/results, viability comparisons and
   discussion. The fabrication recipe is outside this assessment.

3. NONLIVING ROBOT FLUID. Hydraulic power transmission, cooling, or energy storage
   can each be useful without nourishing cells. Aubin et al. (2019) demonstrated
   a soft aquatic robot combining flow-battery energy storage and hydraulic
   actuation. That is a relevant synthetic-circulation precedent, not transfusable
   blood or a demonstrated tissue-compatible perfusate. For a biohybrid body,
   separate mechanical and biological loops may be easier to reason about first.
   https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-019-1313-1
   https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31217583/
   Read depth: original paper abstract and bibliographic record, not full methods.

The 2026 primary model and the scope of our reduction
--------------------------------------------------
Bonart et al. couples channel flow, wall exchange and tissue metabolism, including
blockage, spatial density and axial depletion. Its examples are computational;
PF0 is a smaller independent planar model, not replication of the full network.
The paper's illustrative high-demand values give density 2e14 cells/m3,
q_cell=1e-16 mol/cell/s, qmax=0.02 mol/m3/s, Km=0.028 mol/m3, D=2e-9 m2/s,
free inlet=0.2 mol/m3 and assumed hypoxia threshold=0.05 mol/m3. These are
liver-like benchmark values, not measured skin parameters. Wall permeability
0.05–0.2 mm/s is its example range; PF0 also adds a 0.005 mm/s stress case.

PF0 equations (our reduced model, SI units throughout)
----------------------------------------------------
Within each one-sided slab, 0 < x < H:
  D d2C/dx2 - qmax*C/(Km+C) = 0
  -D*dC/dx at x=0 = P*(C_lumen - C_wall)
  dC/dx at x=H = 0
Along a supplying channel of width W:
  Q * beta * dC_lumen/dz = -W*J_wall

C denotes FREE oxygen, in mol/m3. The deliberately hypothetical equilibrium law
C_total=beta*C_free applies in the lumen only. Increasing beta adds reversible
content at the same free concentration; it never multiplies diffusivity or free
inlet oxygen. This linear law specifies neither a chemical material nor its
affinity, release kinetics, viscosity, oxidation or toxicity. More realistic
binding would replace beta with dC_total/dC_free and require new parameters.

The software uses finite-volume tissue balances, a nonnegative Newton solve,
finite wall resistance, and implicit axial depletion. It integrates delivered
oxygen against tissue uptake. A finite-volume cell minimum is a discretized
field estimate, not a directly measured biological concentration.

Useful analytic rejection tests, derived here
--------------------------------------------
Let qcrit=qmax*Ccrit/(Km+Ccrit). If every cell is to stay above Ccrit, its oxygen
use is at least qcrit. A necessary local condition is therefore:
  Cin - qcrit*(H/P + H^2/(2D)) >= Ccrit.
Failure rejects that slab even with no axial depletion and unlimited flow or
carrier capacity at the same free inlet. An independent necessary supply bound
is Q*beta*(Cin-Ccrit) >= qcrit*W*H*length. Passing either is not sufficient.
Replacing qcrit by qmax gives a conservative zero-order slab lower bound under
a fixed lumen concentration; a negative value signals failure of that bound,
not a prediction of physically negative oxygen.

Executed PF0 result
-------------------
The code and inputs were frozen before the main run. The 144-case sweep used
depths 50/100/200/400 micrometers, four flows, three permeabilities and three
hypothetical capacities. Geometry and sweep ranges are declared modeling choices,
not a prescription for culturing tissue. Results:

  Cases above the assumed threshold: 55/144.
  At beta 1, 4 and 16: respectively 16, 19 and 20 passes out of 48 each.
  Cases failing the local necessary bound: 72/144.
  Maximum relative mass-balance error: 1.82e-8.
  Largest difference on eight doubled-grid checks: 0.0251% of inlet concentration.
  Predeclared checks: 6/7 passed; the failed check is retained.

The important failure is informative: the initial expectation that greater wall
permeability must always improve the global minimum was false for two low-flow,
deep-tissue groups. A separately labeled post-run diagnosis refined six cases
and reproduced both reversals. Increasing permeability improved the fixed-lumen
local profile but increased upstream extraction, reducing downstream oxygen.
For 200-micrometer depth, the refined downstream minimum fell from 0.0009221
to 0.0006676 mol/m3 when P increased from 0.05 to 0.2 mm/s at the fixed low-flow
case. These remain far below the assumed threshold. This is a coupled-model
counterexample; it does not establish a biological effect. The original failed
check has not been converted into a retrospective pass.

Within PF0, flow and capacity enter as Q*beta; their equivalence is a property
of the stipulated law, not evidence for a realizable carrier. Additional carrier
capacity has diminishing value when diffusion distance or wall transfer is the
dominant limit. PF0 has no pump-power, mechanical-deformation, glucose, CO2,
lactate, pH, thermal or tissue-function model. It cannot certify viable skin.

Concrete next step for living tissue over machinery
--------------------------------------------------
Choose a nonneural skin/tissue module and source its actual demand, transport,
thickness and stress ranges before claiming that route is feasible. Extend the
same conservative network to couple oxygen and a waste/nutrient constraint,
then perturb channel compression/occlusion during a prescribed motion cycle.
Compare network redesign against more flow or more carrier content, measuring
the worst local condition and pump cost rather than mean oxygen alone. That
tests a requirement directly created by living tissue on moving robotic parts.
It remains a computational design program until appropriate experimental data
can validate its tissue-specific assumptions.
