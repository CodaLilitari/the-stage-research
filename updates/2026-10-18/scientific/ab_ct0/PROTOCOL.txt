WHY THIS IS THE NEXT LIMITATION
PF0 already couples finite axial supply to a transverse tissue slab, but its
C_total=beta*C_free law makes Q*beta equivalent and ignores viscosity and finite
release. PX1 proves that distal oxygen need not increase with wall transfer in a
finite-supply model. OK0 adds finite release only between clamped baths. MECH1
couples hydraulics to a zero-order territory bound without dynamic carrier
release. The Cowork rectangle supplies the full wall at a clamped concentration
and has no finite axial inventory. AB-CT0 connects these particular missing
pieces while preserving a tractable one-channel, one-wall geometry.

This is a small bridge study, not execution of Lily's 486-case CT1 plan. It fixes
geometry and demand and adds a finite-binding comparison. It does not adopt
CT1's proposed 20% spacing threshold, claim a biological optimum, or select a
material. The future full CT1 plan remains separately preserved.

FALSIFIABLE QUESTIONS AND DECISION RULES
H1: In at least one prespecified Phase A equilibrium case, adding carrier storage
while raising viscosity from its baseline to the fourfold scenario does not
improve distal tissue concentration over the dissolved-only baseline at the
same pressure/power envelope. If no such case occurs, report H1 unsupported on
this matrix; do not broaden it to a universal capacity benefit or invent a new
range after inspecting outcomes. The fixed-flow and matched-viscosity comparisons
separate binding-shape effects from the viscosity/flow change.

H2: In at least one NONZERO finite-rate case, equilibrium binding overstates
integrated tissue uptake. A carrier-versus-dissolved distal rank reversal is a
secondary question and requires all three relevant cases to be refined.
The k=0 negative control cannot by itself satisfy H2. Compare to the
matched equilibrium case. A negative result is useful:
it bounds the consequence within these selected rates. Do not assume equilibrium
is a universal upper bound on distal oxygen; delayed release may redistribute
oxygen along the channel.

H3: At least one matched case increases total uptake but lowers distal tissue
oxygen when wall permeability increases. PX1 supplies a valid reduced-model
counterexample; AB-CT0 may or may not reproduce one in this particular slab and
parameter matrix. Absence does not invalidate PX1, and a reversal is not itself
a numerical failure. Interpret it only after conservation/refinement checks.
H3 searches both Phase A and Phase B. Its matching key is profile, viscosity,
flow mode, dimensional k plus equilibrium/finite flag, geometry, inlet, binding
constitutive settings and demand; ONLY wall permeability varies. In particular,
the shared Phase B profile label must not merge different finite binding rates.

Report continuous matched differences, their numerical refinement uncertainty,
and which assumption changes their sign. Last-grid changes are numerical
diagnostics, not certified mathematical error bounds. No p-values, stochastic confidence
intervals, grid success probabilities, carrier ranking or clinical threshold
are appropriate. Do not call a difference resolved unless its magnitude exceeds
twice the larger last-refinement change for the compared cases. An unresolved
small difference remains unresolved; do not equate it to physical equivalence.

FIXED GEOMETRY AND UNITS
One idealized wide, straight rectangular channel, length L=1e-3 m, wall width
w=1e-3 m, channel gap h_c=1e-4 m. It supplies one planar tissue slab of depth
d=1e-4 m. Exchange area=w*L; tissue volume=w*L*d; channel area A=w*h_c.
Only that wall exchanges oxygen; opposite tissue surface is no-flux. No second
wall, atmospheric source, interstitial convection or hidden multiplicity.
The channel gap is a NEW analyst-selected hydraulic coordinate. PF0 did not
specify it. The wide-slit hydraulic model neglects finite-width edge and entry
losses; this study does not claim a dimensioned manufactured channel network.

Reference values are PF0's illustrative high-demand fixture:
  c_in=0.2 mol/m3; D_t=2e-9 m2/s; Rmax=0.02 mol/m3/s; Km=0.028 mol/m3.
  R(c_t)=Rmax*c_t/(Km+c_t), with c_t in mol/m3.
These are inherited model coordinates, not measured skin or carrier data.
Reference viscosity mu0=7.8e-4 Pa*s is the MECH1 model value. Multipliers and
binding rates below are hypothetical controls, not a measured formulation.
No heme distance, fold confidence or structure is converted into a binding rate.

Hydraulics uses the fully developed Newtonian wide-slit approximation:
  R_h(mu)=12*mu*L/(w*h_c^3); deltaP=R_h*Q; hydraulic_power=R_h*Q^2.
Define the fixed budgets from baseline R0=R_h(mu0):
  deltaP_max=R0*(3 microlitres/min)
  power_max=R0*(2 microlitres/min)^2.
All flows are converted to m3/s BEFORE using these equations. These small
budgets are selected to expose pressure/power tradeoffs at existing PF0-scale
flows; they are not a pump specification or motor/electrical power budget.
  Envelope mode: Q=min(deltaP_max/R_h, sqrt(power_max/R_h)).
  Fixed-flow ablation: Q=2 microlitres/min; report pressure/power violations.
The same module budgets apply to every profile. Do not give a viscous carrier
a larger budget. At multiplier 1 the power cap is active; at multiplier 4 the
pressure cap is active. Verify this algebra before solving.

CARRIER PROFILES — FREE OXYGEN DRIVES THE WALL
Use dissolved free oxygen c in both lumen and tissue, with an explicitly assumed
unit partition coefficient. Bound carrier stays in the lumen. Total luminal
content C_T has units mol oxygen per m3 fluid. Do not diffuse C_T into tissue.

D: dissolved only, C_T=c.
L: hypothetical instantaneous linear storage, C_T=5*c.
H: hypothetical Hill equilibrium, s_eq(c)=c^n/(c^n+c50^n), n=2.7,
   c50=0.5*c_in; C_T=c+B*s_eq(c).
   Choose B=(4*c_in)/s_eq(c_in), in mol/m3 fluid.

L and H have the SAME bound-oxygen inlet inventory, 4*c_in, and total inlet
content 5*c_in. This matches inlet stored oxygen rather than maximum binding
capacity. It differs deliberately from CT1's kappa=B/(alpha*P_in) convention;
do not silently report it as the same CT1 parameter. Always save B, s_in and
actual C_T,in. None of these profiles is identified as HbV, PFC or a product.

Finite-rate H carries saturation s as an independent state:
  ds/dt=k*(s_eq(c)-s), s(0)=s_eq(c_in).
The rate k is an assumed effective relaxation, not a molecular off-rate. Set
k through k*T_ref in {0,0.1,10}, with T_ref=A*L/Q_ref and Q_ref=2 uL/min.
Hold each dimensional k FIXED across viscosity cases; report actual k*A*L/Q.
Do not renormalize k to cancel the residence-time change caused by viscosity.
The k=0 condition carries permanently inaccessible bound oxygen and is a
negative control. The equilibrium model is solved algebraically, not simulated
by an arbitrarily huge k.

COUPLED EQUATIONS
At each axial z, the steady tissue profile satisfies
  D_t*d2c_t/dy2 - R(c_t)=0, 0<y<d;
  -D_t*dc_t/dy at y=0 = J=Pwall*(c-c_t(0)); dc_t/dy at y=d=0.
With y increasing from the wall into tissue, wall influx J is positive.
Across the channel:
  d[Q*(c+B*s)]/dz = -w*J,
  ds/dz = (A/Q)*k*(s_eq(c)-s).
For D/L/equilibrium H, substitute the corresponding C_T(c) in the first equation.
Q is constant along this incompressible one-channel model. Do not import Rhee's
unequal inlet/outlet pump settings or a hidden water leakage term.

A conservative implicit segment for finite H can use a=k*A*dz/Q and
  s_j=(s_previous+a*s_eq(c_j))/(1+a),
  c_j+B*s_j + (w*dz/Q)*J(c_j) = c_previous+B*s_previous.
The scalar residual is monotone for the specified constitutive/tissue laws.
Bracket physical nonnegative free content without silently clipping a negative
state. An implementation can use [0,C_T,previous], then check the stronger
model-specific bounds. Use safeguarded bisection/Newton with bounded iterations;
record every nonlinear or bracketing failure as a result, not a zero field.
For equilibrated inlet and these fixed passive laws, the invariant bounds are
0<=c<=c_in and 0<=s<=s_in. At c=c_in, the implicit residual is nonnegative
when the previous state satisfies those bounds; at c=0 it is nonpositive.
Thus [0,min(c_in,C_T,previous)] is also a valid bracket. Verify these bounds
without clipping. No externally supersaturated carrier is supplied.

REQUIRED ANALYTIC AND CONSERVATION CHECKS
1. B=0 and linear storage recover PF0's beta=1 and beta=5 equations at matched
   Q/geometry. Q*beta degeneracy should hold only for that linear law, not be
   imposed on Hill or finite kinetics or on unmatched hydraulic budgets.
2. k=0 holds s constant; free-oxygen/tissue results equal dissolved-only at the
   SAME Q, even though total inlet content is higher. Bound outlet inventory
   must show the inaccessible storage remaining in the fluid.
3. Zero demand gives uniform free concentration and zero uptake for equilibrated
   inlet carrier. Pwall=0 gives zero wall supply and zero lumen depletion. The
   isolated, zero-demand tissue state is nonunique and must not be invented as
   a uniqueness check. Zero Q is an unsupplied/no-steady-throughflow condition,
   not an epsilon-flow fallback.
4. Large finite k approaches the algebraic equilibrium solution under refinement.
   This is a numerical limit check, not an asserted biological upper bound.
5. For the verification-only linear tissue sink R=k_t*c_t, let gamma=sqrt(k_t/D).
   Define G=[1/Pwall+1/(D*gamma*tanh(gamma*d))]^-1. Then J=G*c and, with
   linear luminal storage beta, c(z)=c_in*exp[-w*G*z/(Q*beta)]. Tissue distal
   minimum is c(z)/[cosh(gamma*d)+(D*gamma/Pwall)*sinh(gamma*d)]. This exact
   coupled slab/axial solution checks both solver pieces together. PX1's exact
   reduced solution is a second independent simple diagnostic, not the same PDE.
6. With positive constant zero-order demand and clamped local lumen supply,
   c_min=c_in-q*(d/Pwall+d^2/(2D)) when the expression stays positive. Do not
   report its negative extrapolation as a physical concentration. At fixed
   local supply, increasing Pwall must not lower the minimum. Do not impose
   this monotonicity on globally coupled finite-flow cases.
7. Independently compare three quantities: Q*(C_T,in-C_T,out), integrated wall
   flux and integrated tissue reaction. Do not define uptake by inlet-minus-
   outlet and then call the equality an independent conservation test. Check
   each axial segment, the full domain, and bound/free inventory separately.
   Integrated uptake cannot exceed Q*C_T,in; with k=0 it cannot exceed Q*c_in.
   Check these available-inventory bounds explicitly, without defining uptake
   from them or hiding a residual through clipping.
8. Nonnegative finite concentrations, 0<=s<=1, bounded input units, consistent
   one-wall exchange area/volume and pressure/power inequalities are required.
   Reject NaN/Inf, negative dimensions/rates/capacity, invalid viscosity and
   unsupported zero-flow states. Units are seconds/metres/moles/Pascals internally.
