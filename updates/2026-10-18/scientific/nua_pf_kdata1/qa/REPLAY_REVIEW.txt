MATERIAL INTERPRETIVE CORRECTIONS
1. Geometry and channel transfer: the groups differ in more than geometry, and this assay does not intervene on geometry while holding every material feature fixed. Replace 'PROVE geometry-dependent' and '~3x overestimate in an encapsulated channel' with a preparation-associated, assay-specific difference. It cautions against transporting a free-Hb coefficient unchanged; it does not quantify the error of a Stage channel coefficient. No viscosity was measured or inferred by this fitting exercise. Stage's separate hydraulic assumptions remain hypothetical.

2. Lag is structurally confounded, not measured zero. Original M2 lags are hHb 1.936765e-10 s, RBC 4.914640e-8 s, PEG-LEH 1.770554e-7 s; local results are 4.035680e-10, 1.080084e-6 and 4.609314e-8 s. All are positive and lie far below their respective first observations. On this branch, dA and tlag occur only through dA*exp(k*tlag). Choosing tlag = half the first observation and dA_new = dA_old*exp(-k*tlag) reproduces all M1 predictions to floating-point precision, without fitting. The amplitude and lag Jacobian columns are exactly dependent. The enormous and environment-sensitive lag SEs reflect this singularity; nominal four-parameter AICc is not a valid regular-model lag test. Nor did the original single near-zero start search exhaustively test a piecewise onset inside the measured interval. Retain 'no independently identified lag in this analysis'; do not claim no physical delay or that lag is zero. The RBC M2 rate differs between runs by about 0.00836 per second with negligible SSE change; that unstable redundant parameterization is not a meaningful new physical result.

3. Correlated residuals and uncertainty: adjacent full-M1 residual correlations are 0.998053 (hHb), 0.989357 (RBC), 0.968961 (LEH). For the later windows they remain 0.998238, 0.986815 and 0.945945. This makes an independent, identically distributed residual interpretation inappropriate. Small covariance SEs must not be promoted to reliable inferential intervals, preparation variability or material uncertainty. No shot-level confidence interval is identifiable from these supplied means alone. Pointwise residual correlation can reflect model misspecification and/or correlated acquisition; this review does not separate them.

4. Windows and timescales: 'full M1' is a fit over the entire stored window, not an early-phase rate. The later windows begin at 0.02875 s (hHb, 886 points), 0.412 s (RBC, 898), and 0.46 s (LEH, 886); their ends are 0.25, 4 and 4 s. These are fractions of unequal source windows, not matched physiological intervals. The later fitted signal spans about 9.73% (RBC) and 2.86% (LEH) of the corresponding full-fit observed-window amplitude, leaving the results especially exposed to plateau/baseline structure. This is evidence of descriptive window sensitivity and residual mismatch; it does not identify two physical timescales, membrane diffusion separately, or a validated time-varying release law. AICc values from different windows must not be compared as model evidence because they use different observations.

5. Replication and source metadata: one supplied averaged curve is not evidence of one biological preparation, pooled donor units, or Hb purified once. Those specific dictionary assertions are not established by the inspected primary source. Table 1 labels the study columns n=12 but does not supply those individual kinetic traces in this workbook. Figure 4 describes averages of 10-15 traces. Keep the supplied data unit, study-level n and missing replicate metadata distinct. The 15 micromolar heme statement describes Hb/PEG-LEH suspensions before mixing, without clearly establishing final postmix concentration or the RBC dilution. Instrument dead time is not stated; do not substitute a typical value as source evidence.

The corresponding rate ratios describe these assay traces, not a causal geometry effect or a channel-transfer factor.
