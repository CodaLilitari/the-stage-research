VERIFIED CONDITIONS AND PUBLISHED RATE CONTEXT
The rapid-kinetics methods and Figure 4 caption support stopped-flow deoxygenation with sodium dithionite, 20 degrees C, 437.5 nm, PBS (0.1 M, pH 7.4), and an average of 10-15 kinetic traces. Methods describe oxygenated Hb/PEG-LEH suspensions at an overall heme concentration of 15 micromolar being mixed with 1.5 mg/mL dithionite solution. The wording does not unambiguously specify final postmix heme concentration or extend the explicit concentration statement to the RBC preparation; preserve that limitation. The material source was expired, leuko-reduced human RBCs. Equilibrium oxygen curves were measured separately at 37 degrees C.

Table 1, visually inspected from the publisher's original large PNG, reports k_off,O2 (per second): human Hb 36.51 +/- 2.53; human RBC 8.61 +/- 2.55; PEG-LEH 11.18 +/- 1.82. These three columns are headed n = 12. The adjacent historical Rameez column has n = 3 and PEG-LEH rate 21.57 per second; it is a different study. The article methods/statistical paragraph and table image inspected here do not define whether the displayed +/- is SD or SE or establish that the 12 units are independent donors. Do not guess those details. A representative averaged trace and the table's across-sample summary are different evidence objects.

WHAT THE WORKBOOK ACTUALLY CONTAINS
Three oxygen sheets concatenate experimental rows labeled Exp and published model rows labeled Fit. Column order differs by sheet, so import by header. Time is in seconds and Value is absorbance (AU), established by Figure 4; the worksheet headers alone omit units.
- O2 Offloading Hbs: 985 Exp points, Excel rows 2-986, times 0.004-0.25 s, step 0.00025 s; 985 Fit points, rows 987-1971.
- O2 Offloading RBCs: 998 Exp points, rows 2-999, times 0.012-4 s, step 0.004 s; 998 Fit points, rows 1000-1997.
- O2 Offloading LEHs: 985 Exp points, rows 2-986, times 0.064-4 s, step 0.004 s; 985 Fit points, rows 987-1971.
There is one published experimental series per oxygen sheet, not the 10-15 individual shot records and not 12 independent experimental series. Fitting both Exp and Fit rows would contaminate a new analysis with the authors' modeled outputs. Counts of time points are not replicate counts.

Norm is a stored transformation, not a direct independently calibrated oxygen saturation. The source record inventory separately checks its consistency with the first/last published Fit values. Three RBC experimental Norm values are negative; 701 RBC, 75 Hb and 619 PEG-LEH experimental Norm values exceed 1. These deviations must not be silently clipped or treated as impossible physical saturation. All underlying oxygen Value entries are positive. The first stored timestamps differ among preparations and do not prove a biological delay.

DISPOSITION OF NUA'S NUMERIC CLAIMS
The proposed 37.2 +/- 0.2 (hHb), 12.6 +/- 0.3 (RBC), 11.8 +/- 0.1 (LEH) per-second values and late rates 4.6/2.2 are UNVERIFIED collaborator-derived fits here. They are not the Table 1 aggregate numbers and should not be labeled as such. Their reproduction awaits the actual analysis package, exact selected Exp rows/windows, model equation, baseline treatment, weighting, bounds and error definition. A disagreement with the table is not automatically an error: these are potentially representative-trace fits under different choices. Conversely, a small fit SE from hundreds of correlated samples of one averaged trace is not biological reproducibility or a confidence interval for a population rate.

INTERPRETATION AND FITTING AMBIGUITIES
1. Treat these as assay-specific apparent/effective offloading rates. For encapsulated Hb, the signal can combine chemical dissociation, intracellular/vesicle diffusion, membrane transfer and external scavenging/mixing. The paper itself discusses diffusion barriers. The data do not uniquely isolate a microscopic heme k_off, membrane permeability or an in vivo delivery constant.
2. A Stage equation dS/dt = k(S_eq(c)-S) needs a separate derivation/calibration before using an apparent stopped-flow fit as k. Even a two-state microscopic binding model relaxes at k_on*c + k_off, which depends on concentration. The source does not measure the full reversible law or establish 37-degree kinetics. Its 37-degree equilibrium measurements cannot silently supply a same-temperature kinetic calibration.
3. The methods describe ln(absorbance) against time, but Figure 4 and the stored Exp values rise toward a nonzero plateau. For A(t) = A_infinity - B*exp(-k*t), the linear decay transform uses ln(A_infinity-A), not ln(A). The short methods description leaves the actual baseline, transformation and fit window under-specified. This is a reproducibility limitation, not proof that the authors' underlying analysis is wrong.
4. Time offset and amplitude can be structurally confounded: after onset, A(t)=A_infinity-B*exp[-k(t-t0)] equals A_infinity-(B*exp(k*t0))*exp(-k*t). If B is free and no pre-onset transition is observed or otherwise constrained, changing t0 can be exactly absorbed into B. A numerically flat lag profile in such a model does not identify a physical lag. An externally fixed amplitude or a piecewise onset could change this conclusion and must be stated explicitly.
5. Whole-trace, early and late fits ask different questions. The experimental RBC and PEG-LEH traces visibly depart from the authors' single fitted curves. A late fitted rate could reflect a slow component, baseline drift, unresolved mixtures or model mismatch. It is not automatically a second molecular dissociation constant. A bounded replay should disclose alternative baseline/window choices and correlated residuals, rather than select one tail and promote its small nominal SE.
