INDEPENDENT LINEAR TWO-STATE BENCHMARK DERIVATION
Coda (Codex Lily) | 17 October 2026
Prepared before receiving Lily's benchmark implementation. This is symbolic
analysis, not a transport run or an empirical carrier model.

Assumptions and units

Use residence time t in seconds. c and b are free and bound oxygen content in
mol/m3 fluid. a>=0 is a CONSTANT free-oxygen extraction rate in s^-1, k>=0 is a
CONSTANT effective exchange rate in s^-1, gamma>=0 is dimensionless, c0>=0,
and b0=gamma*c0. There is no added oxygen along the trajectory:

  b' = k*(gamma*c-b)
  (c+b)' = -a*c
  c' = -(a+k*gamma)*c+k*b.

gamma is a linear equilibrium storage ratio, not a measured binding capacity,
Hill coefficient or material identity. All conclusions below require this
homogeneous linear law and equilibrated inlet. They are not general theorems
about Michaelis-Menten tissue, Hill binding, variable extraction or arbitrary
initial loading.

Eigenvalues and exact solution

Let g=1+gamma, S=a+k*g, and
  delta^2 = S^2-4*a*k
          = (a-k)^2+2*a*k*gamma+k^2*gamma*(2+gamma)
          = (a-k*g)^2+4*a*k*gamma.
The nonnegative decay rates are
  r_fast=(S+delta)/2,
  r_slow=2*a*k/(S+delta),
with eigenvalues -r_slow and -r_fast. The second expression avoids subtracting
nearly equal S and delta. Branch S=0 explicitly before dividing. Algebra gives
r_slow*r_fast=a*k and r_slow+r_fast=S.

For a,k,gamma>0 the roots are distinct and
  0<r_slow<a/g<a<r_fast.
Writing d=r_fast-r_slow=delta,
  c(t)/c0 = w_s*exp(-r_slow*t)+w_f*exp(-r_fast*t),
  w_s=(r_fast-a)/d,  w_f=(a-r_slow)/d,  w_s+w_f=1.
Both weights are nonnegative. This satisfies c(0)=c0 and c'(0)=-a*c0.

The bound content is
  b(t)=gamma*c0*[r_fast*exp(-r_slow*t)-r_slow*exp(-r_fast*t)]/d.
A stable equivalent is
  E(t)=exp(-r_slow*t)*[-expm1(-d*t)]/d,
  b(t)=gamma*c0*[exp(-r_slow*t)+r_slow*E(t)].
The continuous d=0 limit is E(t)=t*exp(-r_slow*t). Branch zero parameters
explicitly; do not form 0*infinity and then rely on cancellation.

Stable weights near a repeated root or a vanishing coefficient

Put x=a-k*g. Since delta^2-x^2=4*a*k*gamma:
  if x>=0: w_s=2*a*k*gamma/[delta*(delta+x)], w_f=1-w_s;
  if x< 0: w_f=2*a*k*gamma/[delta*(delta-x)], w_s=1-w_f.
These avoid subtracting nearly equal positive quantities for the small weight.
Handle gamma=0 separately. Scale rate inputs before squaring/multiplication if
the accepted domain could overflow/underflow; a hypot-style discriminant can
help. A raw exp(-S*t/2)*cosh(delta*t/2) representation risks 0*infinity at long
times even when the true solution is finite. Near-zero d must use an analytic
divided-difference limit, not division by an arbitrary epsilon.

Total content and cumulative extraction

Set alpha=a/g. The normalized total content is another positive mixture:
  [c(t)+b(t)]/(g*c0) = W_s*exp(-r_slow*t)+W_f*exp(-r_fast*t),
  W_s=(r_fast-alpha)/d, W_f=(alpha-r_slow)/d.
W_s+W_f=1 and W_s*r_slow+W_f*r_fast=alpha.
The near-cancellation product identity is
  (alpha-r_slow)*(r_fast-alpha)=a^2*gamma/g^2.
It can rationalize a small total-content weight just as above.

The extracted oxygen CONTENT per fluid volume is
  U(t)=g*c0-c(t)-b(t)=integral_0^t a*c(u) du.
For small t, compute stably as
  U(t)=g*c0*[W_s*(-expm1(-r_slow*t))+W_f*(-expm1(-r_fast*t))].
Do not confuse U [mol/m3] with channel delivery Q*U(T) [mol/s]. Inlet-minus-
outlet and the integral agree analytically; a numerical implementation should
also compare an independently evaluated integral or analytic expression.

Exact invariant/limit checks

1. c(0)=c0; b(0)=gamma*c0; c'(0)=-a*c0; b'(0)=0.
   c''(0)=a*(a+k*gamma)*c0 and b''(0)=-a*k*gamma*c0.
2. c,b>=0; both are nonincreasing for this equilibrated, constant-coefficient
   problem. b>=gamma*c, and 0<=U<=g*c0. For a,k>0, total content tends to zero.
3. k=0: c=c0*exp(-a*t), b=gamma*c0. Only initially free oxygen is accessible;
   U=c0*(1-exp(-a*t))<=c0. High inlet bound inventory is not instant delivery.
4. gamma=0: b=0 and c=c0*exp(-a*t), independent of k. The second eigenmode is
   unexcited by the prescribed inlet and must not affect physical results.
5. a=0: the equilibrated inlet stays c=c0,b=gamma*c0 and U=0. c0=0 gives
   the identically zero solution; avoid normalizing a zero state.
6. Repeated roots in the nonnegative domain occur only with gamma=0,a=k,
   or the all-zero rate degeneracy. For gamma=0,a=k=r and arbitrary b0,
   exp(Mt)=exp(-r*t)*[[1,r*t],[0,1]]. Under the specified b0=0 this reduces
   exactly to c=c0*exp(-r*t),b=0. Positive gamma with positive a,k cannot
   have an exactly repeated eigenvalue, although it can be nearly degenerate.
7. k->infinity, fixed positive t: b->gamma*c and c->c0*exp[-a*t/g].
   There is a fast derivative initial layer with timescale approximately
   1/(k*g): finite-k c'(0)=-a*c0, but the equilibrium-limit derivative is
   -a*c0/g. An equilibrated inlet removes an order-one state jump; the fast
   free-state amplitude is approximately a*gamma/[k*g^2], not zero. Do not
   require derivative convergence at t=0 or declare large finite k identical
   to algebraic equilibrium.

Two useful inequalities — only within the stated linear assumptions

At the SAME residence time, c_finite(t)>=c0*exp(-a*t), because b-gamma*c>=0
and c'=-a*c+k*(b-gamma*c). Thus a passive equilibrated carrier cannot produce
a free-oxygen rank BELOW dissolved-only at the same a and same residence time.
If code shows that, check changed flow, changed a, initial loading or a defect.

Convexity of exp gives
  (c+b)/(g*c0) >= exp(-alpha*t),
because the positive total-content mixture has weighted mean rate alpha.
Consequently U_finite(t)<=g*c0*[1-exp(-alpha*t)]=U_equilibrium(t).
This cumulative-uptake ordering does NOT imply a fixed free-oxygen ordering:
for a,k,gamma>0, finite c initially falls faster than equilibrium c, yet its
slower nonzero decay mode eventually exceeds the equilibrium free concentration.
At least one free-concentration crossing follows. Do not transfer the uptake
inequality to distal/free oxygen, or extrapolate either statement to nonlinear
binding/tissue or a variable extraction coefficient.

Pump-budget rank reversal

Assume the same geometry, inlet free oxygen and extraction coefficient a>0.
Let T_D be dissolved residence time and T_C carrier residence time. If outlet
tissue oxygen equals the same positive constant times outlet free oxygen in
both branches, its ranking is the same. This applies to a common linear slab
coupling; it is not automatic if transfer/geometry changes.

Equilibrium carrier beats dissolved exactly when
  exp[-a*T_C/g] > exp[-a*T_D], i.e. T_C/g<T_D.
At k=0, carrier free oxygen is lower when T_C>T_D. Therefore the strict interval
  1<T_C/T_D<g, equivalently 1/g<Q_C/Q_D<1,
guarantees a rank reversal for sufficiently small NONZERO k by continuity.
This establishes existence in the linear benchmark, not a real material result.

A conservative explicit sufficient rate bound follows from b<=gamma*c0 and
c'<=-a*c+k*gamma*c0:
  0<k<a*[exp(-a*T_D)-exp(-a*T_C)]/
          [gamma*(1-exp(-a*T_C))].
Together with the strict residence-time interval, this ensures finite carrier
free oxygen remains below dissolved while equilibrium carrier is above it.
The bound is sufficient, not an exact critical rate. Avoid evaluating its small
exponential differences with unstable subtraction.

For hydraulic resistance R_C=m*R_D and common limits:
  Q(R)=min(deltaP_max/R, sqrt(power_max/R)).
Pressure-only operation gives Q_C/Q_D=1/m; power-only gives1/sqrt(m). With a
cap switch, compute the min separately for each branch; do not mix fixed-flow
and envelope comparisons. For the inherited illustrative Q_D=2,Q_C=0.75 and
gamma=4, 1/5<0.75/2<1 satisfies the existence interval. This is pure arithmetic,
not evaluation of a transport case, and the linear law differs from AB-CT0 Hill.

Equal pump budgets do not mean equal inlet TOTAL oxygen flux, equal oxygenator
replenishment cost or equal carrier-production cost. Report Q, T, a, gamma, k,
inlet free/bound/total contents and Q*U(T) separately. Keep dimensional k fixed
as flow changes; resetting k*T to a common value removes the intended kinetic
residence-time effect.
