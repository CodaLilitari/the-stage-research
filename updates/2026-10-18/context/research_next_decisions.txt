Artificial-blood modeling is the near-term priority. Human oxygen carriers and
circulation for tissue on a robot remain separate applications. These questions
select model assumptions and missing measurements, not a clinical product or
material. Admission criteria below are proposals to freeze before further work.

What the completed result actually establishes

In one refined hypothetical AB-CT0 comparison under the same pressure/power caps,
equilibrium carrier distal oxygen is 0.4862% above dissolved-only, whereas the
same carrier with finite release is 19.9763% below. These distal contrasts pass
the declared grid-change guard. The tiny equilibrium-minus-dissolved integrated
uptake difference remains unresolved. The comparison uses different flows and
actual powers: dissolved 2 uL/min and 10.4 pW; carriers 0.75 uL/min and 5.85 pW.
Equal permitted caps are not equal actual power. All inputs are hypothetical and
the inlet is clamped; finite axial supply is not a finite recirculating reservoir.

1. Can measured carrier kinetics distinguish equilibrium from finite release?
   NEAR TERM — artificial-blood mathematical/empirical track.

Decision: retain equilibrium, admit a constant effective relaxation rate, or
require a more explicit kinetic/transport model. The selected fixture's 30 s
relaxation and 8 s carrier residence show why kinetics can matter; they do not
estimate a real carrier's rate.

Data needed beyond Nua's completed stopped-flow reanalysis: time-resolved loading/unloading
traces, their measured observable, oxygen boundary change, mixing/dead time,
instrument response, medium, temperature and pH, plus matched equilibrium and
capacity information where available. Preserve source units and uncertainty.
An equilibrium curve alone cannot identify a rate. A molecular off-rate alone
cannot identify effective release through a carrier, fluid and channel boundary.

Admission/stop: first establish whether the observable and apparatus permit
identifying kinetics at all. Admit a constant-rate model only if one parameter
set predicts held-out conditions within a predeclared uncertainty criterion;
separate loading/unloading behavior or systematic multi-timescale residuals
require revising the model. If instrument or external transport dominates,
retain a bounded effective description and stop claiming intrinsic kinetics.
The independent linear benchmark addresses numerical correctness in its linear
limit only; it cannot validate the nonlinear Hill law, empirical rates or biology.
Nua's received traces establish preparation-associated, window-sensitive assay
rates, not the effective channel k. The lag model is structurally confounded and
the residuals strongly correlated. Lily's independent benchmark and the separate
Coda recovery of Wren's supplied-model cases are complete within their stated
envelopes; neither closes the empirical calibration gap.

2. Does the distal ranking survive measured hydraulic losses and uncertainty?
   NEAR TERM — artificial-blood transport decision, after usable kinetics.

Decision: determine whether a carrier advantage/disadvantage is robust under a
specified delivery budget, or remains an assumption-sensitive numerical example.

Data needed: viscosity versus relevant shear/temperature, pressure-flow behavior
for the same fluid and geometry, and additional tubing/connector losses. A claim
about electrical power additionally needs pump efficiency and operating limits;
modeled fluid power is insufficient. Preserve the measured kinetic conditions.
Any finite-reservoir extension needs separate storage/oxygenator boundary data.

Admission/stop: predeclare the comparison constraint and separate distal oxygen
from integrated uptake. Admit a signed ranking only if it survives the supported
parameter/measurement range and the numerical-resolution criterion. If a sign
crosses, or the effect is below either uncertainty scale, report the ranking as
unresolved. Do not select a real material from hypothetical viscosity/rate pairs
or extrapolate the present clamped inlet to a closed-loop device.

3. Does deformation primarily change hydraulic patency, oxygen geometry, or both?
   SEPARATE LATER TRACK — perfused tissue on robotics.

Decision: choose a measured deformation-dependent resistance approximation or
require transient, coupled deformation/flow/transport before interpreting motion.

Data needed: synchronized curvature/strain, channel cross-section, actual flow
and pressure, and registered local free-oxygen observations in the same construct.
State the controller (fixed flow, pressure or power), observed region, resolution,
and sensor/motion artifacts. Grigoryan's located compression/flow-redirection and
RBC-saturation package is a benchmark candidate, not a consuming-tissue minimum;
its raw archive remains uninspected. Mori's stretched perfused skin morphology
and Golub's local physiological oxygen transients answer different questions.

Admission/stop: admit a quasi-static resistance approximation only if it predicts
held-out deformation states and local oxygen within declared uncertainty without
unexplained hysteresis or collapse. Otherwise require an explicitly transient
model or leave the mechanism unresolved. Outlet saturation, dye flow or histology
alone cannot admit a worst-region tissue-oxygen claim. Stop at the observable
actually measured; the bounded prior-art search establishes no novelty priority.
